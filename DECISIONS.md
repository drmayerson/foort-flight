## G0 — repository, vendoring, WebGPU capability

- 2026-11-16 — **Branches.** Work is on `g0-scaffold`; `main` stays empty until the user approves checkpoint G0 (plan Section 0: merge to `main` only at approved checkpoints). Nothing is pushed without the user's go-ahead.
- 2026-11-16 — **Vendoring from a commit, not the working tree.** `tools/sync_foort_gpu.sh [src] [commit]` uses `git archive`, so uncommitted FOORT-GPU work (there was some on 2026-11-16) is never vendored. The snapshot is FOORT-GPU `7896677` (2026-11-16 20:32), recorded in `vendor/foort-gpu/VERSION`. The script keeps the hand-written `vendor/foort-gpu/README.md` and prints the changed files.
- 2026-11-16 — **What is vendored, beyond the plan's table** (Section 2):
  - all of `js/metrics/`, not only Kerr and ST3Cr: `registry.js` imports every metric, and the plan forbids editing vendored files;
  - `js/gpu/traceConfig.js` and `js/gpu/tiers.js`: `js/ref/inspectWorker.js` imports `traceConfig.js`, and `tiers.js` holds the quality-tier settings the plan asks to carry over;
  - `js/sky.js`: the sky picture list with its credits;
  - the M0 WebGPU benchmark (`bench/kerrBench.wgsl` and its three JS files): FOORT-GPU's only WGSL so far, a starting point for the G1 port.
  - Not vendored: the placeholder noise field, `fits.js`, the WebGL2 backend, UI and tests.
- 2026-11-16 — **Data stays under `vendor/`.** The plan's layout lists top-level `noise/` and `sky/` folders "(vendored data, or symlinks)". Copies would double the repository's 34 MB, and symlinks are unreliable on Windows/Dropbox and on GitHub Pages; the game loads `vendor/foort-gpu/noise/…` and `vendor/foort-gpu/sky/…` directly.
- 2026-11-16 — **Relative paths kept.** The vendored files keep FOORT-GPU's relative layout (`vendor/foort-gpu/js/…`, `…/shaders/glsl/…`), so their relative imports work unchanged in the browser and in Node (the unit tests import the registry, units and camera from there).
- 2026-11-16 — **WebGPU device** (`js/gpu/device.js`): high-performance adapter; the limits of plan Section 3 (storage-binding and buffer size, invocations and workgroup sizes, storage buffers/textures per stage, colour attachments) are requested at the adapter's maximum, and if `requestDevice` rejects them it is retried with the defaults (the page reports which were refused). Optional features `timestamp-query`, `shader-f16`, `float32-filterable` are enabled when offered. A failure returns `{ok: false, kind, reason}`; `noWebGPUMessage` turns it into the explanation page with a link to the FOORT-GPU viewer (plan Section 3).
- 2026-11-16 — **GPU timing without stalls** (`js/gpu/timing.js`, `GpuTimer`): a ring of 4 timestamp query sets, resolved at the end of the frame's encoder and mapped asynchronously; when all 4 are still mapping, the frame is not timed (counted as dropped) rather than waited for. Without `timestamp-query` the scheduler falls back to the frame interval.
- 2026-11-16 — **Latency harness** (`latency.html`, `js/gpu/latency.js`). The workload is a synthetic f32 loop (a smooth nonlinear map, ray-tracer-like ALU load), not the Kerr tracer: G0 measures the platform (submission and wait costs, frame pacing), not the tracer, which is G1's job. Chunks are calibrated to ≈ 2 ms of GPU time each by timestamp queries (or wall time). The budget mode is a first version of the plan 5.4 scheduler: +1 chunk while the GPU frame time is < 80% of the budget, ×0.8 above it.
- 2026-11-16 — **Dev tooling** as in FOORT-GPU: `dev/` with Vitest, Playwright (installed Chrome, `--enable-unsafe-webgpu`), `tsc --checkJs` with `@webgpu/types`; the Node static server `dev/scripts/serve.mjs` (copied from FOORT-GPU) on port 8766 (FOORT-GPU's tests use 8765). `createGpu` returns a union discriminated by `ok`, with a type guard `gpuFailed` (the type check runs non-strict, where boolean literals do not narrow).
- 2026-11-16 — **First finding (this development machine, Chrome 157 headless and headed, AMD RDNA 3 iGPU):** a CPU wait for one submission costs ≈ 8 ms (onSubmittedWorkDone and a 16-byte mapAsync alike), so waiting per 2 ms chunk is 4× slower than submitting without waiting. The plan's "never wait on the CPU per chunk" therefore applies to Chrome too, not only to Firefox. Details in `PROGRESS.md`.

### G0 device round (2026-11-16)

- 2026-11-16 — **G0 merged to `main` and pushed** (user: "Merge and push"); GitHub Pages serves it at `drmayerson.github.io/foort-flight/`. `g0-scaffold` is pushed too. Fixes of this round are committed on `g0-scaffold` and need another merge to reach the phone.
- 2026-11-16 — **Firefox's CPU wait is ≈ 100 ms per submission on the laptop (Firefox 160), Chrome's 8 ms.** The no-wait frame loop runs at 60 fps in both. Rule for all later milestones: no GPU wait or synchronous read-back in the per-frame path; every read-back is asynchronous and may arrive ≈ 6 frames late in Firefox; the scheduler uses timestamps that are several frames old.

Reason: the phone (Xclipse 920, Chrome 157) returned no core adapter; Chrome can expose compatibility mode on OpenGL ES devices. A compatibility adapter is recognised by the absence of `core-features-and-limits`, reported as `featureLevel`. Whether the game can run in compatibility mode at all (storage buffers in fragment shaders, texture limits) is decided after the phone's second run.

### G0b — WebGL2 alternative (2026-11-16)

- 2026-11-16 — **Question reopened by the user:** "Do we really need WebGPU (vs what foort-gpu used)?" The plan's Section 3 (WebGPU-first, no WebGL2 fallback) is on hold until a WebGL2 frame-loop test has run on the laptop (Chrome, Firefox) and the phone (user: "Yes, test WebGL2"). Arguments: WebGL2 reuses FOORT-GPU's validated GLSL tracer (most of G1 disappears), runs on the phone, and has no 100 ms Firefox wait; it has no GPU timer queries in Firefox or on the phone, and fewer layout options (8 attachments, no storage buffers).
- 2026-11-16 — **WebGL2 harness** (`js/gl/latencyGL.js`, run by `latency.html` after the WebGPU part, or alone when WebGPU is missing): the busy loop of the WebGPU test in a fragment shader writing 6 RGBA32F attachments; fences polled on MessageChannel yields (no 4 ms setTimeout clamp); chunk cost from (T(16) − T(4))/12 so the fence latency drops out; budget mode driven by fence lag and frame interval only, so it represents the devices without timer queries.
- 2026-11-16 — **G0b device runs:** WebGL2 works on all three (laptop Chrome and Firefox, phone), at 60 fps on the laptop in both browsers with a fence-only budget; the phone has no WebGPU at all. Firefox's WebGL2 waits cost 5 ms (WebGPU: 101 ms). The phone ran at a flat 24 fps with 0 frames in flight, a display or power policy, not GPU load. Recommendation to the user: switch Stage 2A to WebGL2 (decision pending). The budget controller's interval rule (> 20 ms means overload) is wrong on displays below 60 Hz; overload is judged from frames in flight.

The plan is now v1.1 (Sections 1.2, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11; the copy in the user's Downloads is still v1.0).
- 2026-11-16 — **F11: any modern browser with WebGL2**, phones included (follows from F12).
- 2026-11-16 — **Landing page** checks WebGL2 (`js/gl/context.js`): float render targets, ≥ 6 draw buffers, highp fragment floats; otherwise an explanation with the FOORT-GPU link. `caps.html` and `latency.html` keep their WebGPU parts as a record of G0 and for a later WebGPU backend; `js/gpu/` stays for that reason.
- 2026-11-16 — **G1 camera without shader edits (to verify in G1):** FOORT-GPU's `common.glsl` builds each ray as a pinhole projection, pflat = (−1, −r_c/D, −β/(r_c D), α/(r_c sin θ_c D)), mapped by the vielbein uniform `uE`. A general tetrad, a rotation and the r_c, r_c sin θ_c factors can all be folded into `uE`, so cube faces (90° square screens) and the ship's screen should need no change to the vendored GLSL.

## G1 — WebGL2 tracer integration, static observer

- 2026-11-18 — **Branch `g1-tracer`** from `main` at `8a32bcd` (user: "Start G1").
- 2026-11-18 — **Own backend, FOORT-GPU's shaders and setup.** `js/gl/tracer.js` compiles the vendored GLSL tracer unchanged (`trace_kerr_analytic`, `trace` with `G3_EQUALS_G4` / `TEXEL_REUSE` / `BS3`, the generated closed forms) and takes its uniforms from the vendored `traceConfig.gpuTraceSetup`; tables and fluid come from the vendored bake worker (`BakeClient`). FOORT-GPU's `backendWebGL2.js` is **not** vendored: it is tied to the viewer (progressive levels, record cache, compare mode, display and post passes) and waits on a fence after every tile, which plan Section 3 forbids. Its program assembly, table packing and asynchronous record read-back are re-implemented in a few lines, with a pointer to the source. Not supported in the game: ray compaction (8 attachments) and half-float tables (both off by default in FOORT-GPU).
- 2026-11-18 — **Camera through `uE` only, verified.** `js/camera/generalTetrad.js`: columns E0 = u, E1 = −f, E2 = −r_c·up, E3 = r_c sin δ_c·right turn FOORT's pinhole into p = −u + (r_c f + α right + β up)/D for any observer frame (u; f, right, up), so general observers, attitudes, boosts and the six cube faces need no shader change. For FOORT's static observer (f = −e_r, up = −e_θ, right = e_φ) it reproduces `TetradCamera.fromStaticObserver`'s vielbein to float32 storage (unit test). `withCamera` swaps the camera uniforms of a `gpuTraceSetup` and rescales the BS3 tolerance to the camera's pixel angle. `boostFrame` (pure boost, no Thomas rotation) and `rotateFrame` serve G2/G3.
- 2026-11-18 — **Per-frame scheduler** (`js/gl/scheduler.js`): a ray budget R per frame; chunks are segments of 16-row bands taken centre-out, at most 65536 rays each (watchdog). With timer queries R = rate × 0.6 × refresh interval; without, R grows ×1.15 after 3 calm frames (≤ 1 in flight, interval < 1.25 × refresh) and shrinks ×0.7 when 2 frames are in flight or the interval exceeds 1.5 × refresh. The refresh interval is the 20th percentile of the last 120 frame intervals, so 24, 60 and 120 Hz displays all work (G0b phone). A job resolves when the fence after its last chunk has signalled.
- 2026-11-18 — **Vendoring:** `js/gpu/refine.js` added to the snapshot (same commit `7896677`): the vendored `tiers.js` and `traceConfig.js` import its types. `tools/sync_foort_gpu.sh` now replaces the folder's contents instead of the folder (Windows refused to delete a folder in use, which left a half-deleted snapshot; restored from git) and checks the copy against the snapshot.
- 2026-11-18 — **Comparison against FOORT-GPU** (`dev/scripts/g1Compare.mjs`) uses the FOORT-GPU clone next to this repository read-only: its `gpuRunner.mjs` (the viewer's test hook) and `gateConfigs.mjs` (the 13 quality-gate scenes and the tier configurations). It refuses to run unless that clone is at the vendored commit with no local changes in `js/` and `shaders/`. Our page opens in the browser FOORT-GPU's runner launched (two Playwright installations cannot be loaded in one process).
- 2026-11-18 — **Debug display** (`shaders/glsl/debug.glsl`): four-colour screen (turned by the azimuth, an exact symmetry, so the page animates while tracing), first-crossing redshift, steps, termination; untraced pixels grey. The disc emission display arrives with G2.
- 2026-11-18 — **Long fuzzball rays, provisional choice: option 1, game-side only** (user: "not sure about this decision to be honest; so maybe for now option 1"). In G3, long rays are split across frames with FOORT-GPU's existing compaction modes, without changing its shader: step-limited passes (mode 1, state in attachments 6–7), an asynchronous read-back of the unfinished rays, an index texture, resume passes (mode 2) into a second record set, and a merge pass of the game's own. Kept open: if the machinery proves heavy or slow, the alternative is a small "resume in place" mode upstream in FOORT-GPU (option 2), proposed to the user first as an upstream note.

Milestones now run back to back: each still ends with tests, a PROGRESS report, a commit and a merge to `main` with a push; the next milestone starts at once on a new branch. ## G2 — General observers and direct rendering

- 2026-11-18 — **Branch `g2-observers`** from `main` at `ad32211`, started without a stop (working agreement above).
- 2026-11-18 — **Ship coordinates** (`js/physics/`): Kerr in Cartesian Kerr–Schild exactly as plan 4.2 (the sign conventions checked: J^T g_BL J = g_KS to 1e-11, det g = −1, finite inside the horizon and on the axis); the BL map uses the closed-form integrals of 2r/Δ and a/Δ, and an analytic Jacobian (checked against differences). ST3Cr in the base's Cartesian coordinates, built from the vendored g_dd: N = −g_tt, P = g_rr, W = ω/(x² + y²) with θ kept 1e-9 off the axis as the vendored metrics do; equal to J^T g_sph J to 1e-10 and conformally flat (g_θθ = r² g_rr) as the plan states. `createSpacetime` bundles the two coordinate systems and the vector maps.
- 2026-11-18 — **Observers** (`js/physics/observers.js`): ZAMO, static (absent in the ergoregion, with the plan's explanation as the reason), circular geodesics on the equator (Ω from 4th-order differences of ∂_r g; prograde = the larger Ω). The base frame orthonormalises (φ, r, θ) against u in FOORT's Gram–Schmidt order, so a static observer's base frame is FOORT's camera frame. Attitude = yaw, pitch, roll in the frame's own (f, right, up) basis. A free-mode state is built in tracer coordinates, then stored in ship coordinates (`observerState`) and mapped back for the camera (`shipCamera`), so the game's ship → tracer path is the one exercised; for a static observer it reproduces FOORT's vielbein to float32 storage (unit test).
- 2026-11-18 — **Ship-frame redshift in the display**, not in the tracer: s = 1/p_t of the pixel's ray (p·u = 1 for the pinhole camera), recomputed in `display.glsl` from the camera uniforms exactly as `common.glsl` builds the ray; every crossing's g becomes s·g, the sky's shift is s. So the vendored tracer and its records stay FOORT's (g_∞), and a cubemap (G3) can re-use records under a different observer.
- 2026-11-18 — **The game's display shader** (`shaders/glsl/display.glsl`): FOORT-GPU's emission formula (Johnson-SU, redshift power, fudge, noise with slow light and coverage guard, binned tail) ported, plus the ship factor; FOORT-GPU's `display.glsl` itself is not used (it has no ship factor and many viewer-only views).
- 2026-11-18 — **Disc defaults:** FOORT-GPU's B3 comparison disc (Johnson-SU μ = 3, γ = −1.5, σ = 1 in the circumferential radius; banded fluid), the same for Kerr and the fuzzball; noise field "auto" (vendored `autoNoiseField`), σ_n = 0.6 as FOORT-GPU's default.
- 2026-11-18 — **Blackbody mode (F6 default):** T_em = T₀ (R/6 M)^(−3/4) with R the envelope radius, T₀ = 4500 K by default (the disc then spans about 2500–15000 K once shifted, red to blue-white); observed T = s g T_em; weight F (s g)⁴ J X; colour = Planck chromaticity (CIE 1931 fit of Wyman et al. 2013, sRGB, luminance-normalised, desaturated into the gamut), 256 entries in log T from 800 to 60000 K. The tail has no per-crossing g: it uses s⁴ W and T_em s (approximation, documented in the shader).
- 2026-11-18 — **Tone and exposure:** HDR (RGBA16F) → ACES filmic (Narkowicz fit) → sRGB. Auto-exposure from the HDR image's mean luminance (its 1 × 1 mipmap, read back asynchronously every 100 ms), key 0.06 (0.18 burnt the disc out: half the view is usually shadow and black sky), adapting over ≈ 0.5 s. The colour-map mode keeps FOORT-GPU's exposure and soft shoulder.
- 2026-11-18 — **Sky:** procedural stars (three cell layers on a cube over the sphere, at most one star per cell, temperatures 2600–28600 K, point-like: no lensing magnification, plan 4.5), a faint 5000 K glow band; shifted to s T and brightened by s⁴. Pictures (vendored, hung behind the hole as in FOORT-GPU) get s⁴ and a 6500 K → 6500 s K tint (approximate, plan 4.5).
- 2026-11-18 — **Trace boundary 100 M** for every spacetime (FOORT-GPU: max(1.01 r_cam, 60 M) for black holes, 1.01 r_cam for the fuzzball), so escape directions are close to those at infinity for the sky in all directions; the fuzzball's tables then span to 110 M.
