- 2026-11-16 — **GPU timing without stalls** (`js/gpu/timing.js`, `GpuTimer`): a ring of 4 timestamp query sets, resolved at the end of the frame's encoder and mapped asynchronously; when all 4 are still mapping, the frame is not timed (counted as dropped) rather than waited for. Without `timestamp-query` the scheduler falls back to the frame interval.
- 2026-11-16 — **Latency harness** (`latency.html`, `js/gpu/latency.js`). The workload is a synthetic f32 loop (a smooth nonlinear map, ray-tracer-like ALU load), not the Kerr tracer: G0 measures the platform (submission and wait costs, frame pacing), not the tracer, which is G1's job. Chunks are calibrated to ≈ 2 ms of GPU time each by timestamp queries (or wall time). The budget mode is a first version of the plan 5.4 scheduler: +1 chunk while the GPU frame time is < 80% of the budget, ×0.8 above it.
- 2026-11-16 — **Dev tooling** as in FOORT-GPU: `dev/` with Vitest, Playwright (installed Chrome, `--enable-unsafe-webgpu`), `tsc --checkJs` with `@webgpu/types`; the Node static server `dev/scripts/serve.mjs` (copied from FOORT-GPU) on port 8766 (FOORT-GPU's tests use 8765). `createGpu` returns a union discriminated by `ok`, with a type guard `gpuFailed` (the type check runs non-strict, where boolean literals do not narrow).
- 2026-11-16 — **First finding (this development machine, Chrome 157 headless and headed, AMD RDNA 3 iGPU):** a CPU wait for one submission costs ≈ 8 ms (onSubmittedWorkDone and a 16-byte mapAsync alike), so waiting per 2 ms chunk is 4× slower than submitting without waiting. The plan's "never wait on the CPU per chunk" therefore applies to Chrome too, not only to Firefox. Details in `PROGRESS.md`.

- 2026-11-18 — **Camera through `uE` only, verified.** `js/camera/generalTetrad.js`: columns E0 = u, E1 = −f, E2 = −r_c·up, E3 = r_c sin δ_c·right turn FOORT's pinhole into p = −u + (r_c f + α right + β up)/D for any observer frame (u; f, right, up), so general observers, attitudes, boosts and the six cube faces need no shader change. - 2026-11-18 — **Per-frame scheduler** (`js/gl/scheduler.js`): a ray budget R per frame; chunks are segments of 16-row bands taken centre-out, at most 65536 rays each (watchdog). With timer queries R = rate × 0.6 × refresh interval; without, R grows ×1.15 after 3 calm frames (≤ 1 in flight, interval < 1.25 × refresh) and shrinks ×0.7 when 2 frames are in flight or the interval exceeds 1.5 × refresh. The refresh interval is the 20th percentile of the last 120 frame intervals, so 24, 60 and 120 Hz displays all work (G0b phone). A job resolves when the fence after its last chunk has signalled.

## G2 — General observers and direct rendering

- 2026-11-18 — **Branch `g2-observers`** from `main` at `ad32211`, started without a stop (working agreement above).
- 2026-11-18 — **Ship coordinates** (`js/physics/`): Kerr in Cartesian Kerr–Schild exactly as plan 4.2 (the sign conventions checked: J^T g_BL J = g_KS to 1e-11, det g = −1, finite inside the horizon and on the axis); the BL map uses the closed-form integrals of 2r/Δ and a/Δ, and an analytic Jacobian (checked against differences). ST3Cr in the base's Cartesian coordinates, built from the vendored g_dd: N = −g_tt, P = g_rr, W = ω/(x² + y²) with θ kept 1e-9 off the axis as the vendored metrics do; equal to J^T g_sph J to 1e-10 and conformally flat (g_θθ = r² g_rr) as the plan states. `createSpacetime` bundles the two coordinate systems and the vector maps.
- 2026-11-18 — **Observers** (`js/physics/observers.js`): ZAMO, static (absent in the ergoregion, with the plan's explanation as the reason), circular geodesics on the equator (Ω from 4th-order differences of ∂_r g; prograde = the larger Ω). The base frame orthonormalises (φ, r, θ) against u in FOORT's Gram–Schmidt order, so a static observer's base frame is FOORT's camera frame. Attitude = yaw, pitch, roll in the frame's own (f, right, up) basis. A free-mode state is built in tracer coordinates, then stored in ship coordinates (`observerState`) and mapped back for the camera (`shipCamera`), so the game's ship → tracer path is the one exercised; for a static observer it reproduces FOORT's vielbein to float32 storage (unit test).
- 2026-11-18 — **Ship-frame redshift in the display**, not in the tracer: s = 1/p_t of the pixel's ray (p·u = 1 for the pinhole camera), recomputed in `display.glsl` from the camera uniforms exactly as `common.glsl` builds the ray; every crossing's g becomes s·g, the sky's shift is s. So the vendored tracer and its records stay FOORT's (g_∞), and a cubemap (G3) can re-use records under a different observer.
- 2026-11-18 — **The game's display shader** (`shaders/glsl/display.glsl`): FOORT-GPU's emission formula (Johnson-SU, redshift power, fudge, noise with slow light and coverage guard, binned tail) ported, plus the ship factor; FOORT-GPU's `display.glsl` itself is not used (it has no ship factor and many viewer-only views).
- 2026-11-18 — **Disc defaults:** FOORT-GPU's B3 comparison disc (Johnson-SU μ = 3, γ = −1.5, σ = 1 in the circumferential radius; banded fluid), the same for Kerr and the fuzzball; noise field "auto" (vendored `autoNoiseField`), σ_n = 0.6 as FOORT-GPU's default.
- 2026-11-18 — **Blackbody mode (F6 default):** T_em = T₀ (R/6 M)^(−3/4) with R the envelope radius, T₀ = 4500 K by default (the disc then spans about 2500–15000 K once shifted, red to blue-white); observed T = s g T_em; weight F (s g)⁴ J X; colour = Planck chromaticity (CIE 1931 fit of Wyman et al. 2013, sRGB, luminance-normalised, desaturated into the gamut), 256 entries in log T from 800 to 60000 K. The tail has no per-crossing g: it uses s⁴ W and T_em s (approximation, documented in the shader).
- 2026-11-18 — **Tone and exposure:** HDR (RGBA16F) → ACES filmic (Narkowicz fit) → sRGB. Auto-exposure from the HDR image's mean luminance (its 1 × 1 mipmap, read back asynchronously every 100 ms), key 0.06 (0.18 burnt the disc out: half the view is usually shadow and black sky), adapting over ≈ 0.5 s. The colour-map mode keeps FOORT-GPU's exposure and soft shoulder.
- 2026-11-18 — **Sky:** procedural stars (three cell layers on a cube over the sphere, at most one star per cell, temperatures 2600–28600 K, point-like: no lensing magnification, plan 4.5), a faint 5000 K glow band; shifted to s T and brightened by s⁴. Pictures (vendored, hung behind the hole as in FOORT-GPU) get s⁴ and a 6500 K → 6500 s K tint (approximate, plan 4.5).
- 2026-11-18 — **Trace boundary 100 M** for every spacetime (FOORT-GPU: max(1.01 r_cam, 60 M) for black holes, 1.01 r_cam for the fuzzball), so escape directions are close to those at infinity for the sky in all directions; the fuzzball's tables then span to 110 M.

## G3 — Cubemap renderer

- 2026-11-18 — **Branch `g3-cubemap`** from `main` at `cfe3aeb`, started without a stop.
- 2026-11-18 — **Atlas:** a cubemap is one record target of 3N × 2N (face k at ((k mod 3) N, (k div 3) N)); faces front, back, right, left, up, down of the reference frame (`cubeFaceFrames`), each a 90° pinhole. The vendored tracer computes a pixel's screen point from its atlas pixel and the atlas size, so each face's `uScreen` is rescaled (centre and size) to map its N × N block onto [−r_c, r_c]²: no shader change, one target, one bind for the display.
- 2026-11-18 — **Reference observer and display map** (`js/physics/refFrame.js`): the reference frame is the ZAMO frame at the ship boosted by the ship's ZAMO-frame velocity (plan 5.2). Per frame, one 4×4 matrix M = (reference ← ZAMO at the reference) · (ZAMO at the ship ← ship) maps the ray components; the two ZAMO frames are identified (exact for equal (r, θ) by the t and φ symmetries, otherwise the staleness). t and φ: the display uses t_ship as the observer time and φ_ship − φ_ref as an azimuth added to every recorded φ. Ship factor: s = 1/(E_ref p_t) with p_t of the pixel's own reference-frame direction (not the texel's), so s is exact at every pixel.
- 2026-11-18 — **Texel blending:** the emission of the 4 nearest texels is computed and blended (plan: records are never blended); texels at a face edge are clamped to the face (a small seam effect). Stars: escape directions are interpolated and the star field evaluated once, unless the four directions span more than 0.25 rad (a lensing discontinuity), where colours are blended instead (4 texel angles left visible zig-zags in lensed regions).
- 2026-11-18 — **Refresh policy:** a background loop traces cubemaps one after another inside the frame budget (double-buffered: the display keeps the last complete one; atlases are pooled); the first cubemap of a scene is coarse (N/4); while the staleness is below 1e-5 in position and speed the loop idles (turning, orbiting at constant r and θ need no refresh). Not implemented yet: priority by a pixel-error estimate from texel Jacobians, cross-fading between cubemaps (optional in the plan); the staleness measure (relative displacement in (r, θ), relative speed) is shown in the HUD.
- 2026-11-18 — **Long rays split across frames (option 1, as chosen at G1):** for the fuzzball (λ > 0) the faces are traced with FOORT-GPU's `+compact` program into 8-attachment atlases: rays stop after n₁ = 600 steps; an asynchronous read-back of attachment 0 lists the unfinished rays (codes 8/9) per face; they resume in a compact image (256 wide, mode 2, the limit raised by n₁ each round, per face with its own camera uniforms, `uImg` = the atlas size) and are scattered back by a point pass of the game's own. Exact: on a fuzzball cubemap all record cores (termination, steps, escape, 3 crossings) are bit-identical to a single pass; 0.06% of pixels differ in the tail centroids' rounding (FOORT-GPU's documented phase-boundary effect). Not used for black holes (their rays are short; BS3 also loses its step history at a resume).
- 2026-11-18 — **WebGL detail found:** the `+compact` program's integer sampler `uIndex` defaults to texture unit 0, shared with the float sampler `uTables`; WebGL then refuses the draw without an error the page sees (the first fuzzball cubemaps were empty). Unused, it now points at unit 15.
- 2026-11-18 — **Validation method** (`dev/scripts/g3Compare.mjs`): a fresh cubemap at exactly the observer's state vs a direct trace of the same camera, both drawn raw at N × N: emission L1 (FOORT colour-map intensity), s, and the sky error in screen pixels (angle between escape directions over the pixel angle) on pixels both call escaped.
- 2026-11-18 — **No BS3 in the game** (`gameTier` in `js/render/defaults.js`): the game's tiers are FOORT-GPU's without the error-controlled integrator, so Normal = FOORT-GPU's High (RK4, larger steps beyond 20 M). Reason: FOORT-GPU calibrated BS3 (tolerance 0.1 pixel angle) for a far camera with a narrow field (r = 1000 M, under 1°); for a camera near the hole with a 70° field the G3 comparison showed escape directions differing from RK4 by about a screen pixel (median 0.7–1.1 px, at every face size), against 0.3–0.5 px between an RK4 cubemap and an RK4 direct trace. The speed-up was 1.19×; a Kerr cubemap takes 0.1–0.25 s without it. This deviates from plan 5.4 ("Normal: FOORT-GPU's error-controlled RK at tolerance 0.1").

## G5 — Ship dynamics

- 2026-11-18 — **Branch `g5-ship`** from `main` at `d55957b`.
- 2026-11-18 — **Integrator** (`js/physics/ship.js`): Dormand–Prince 5(4) (FSAL) in proper time over the 20-component state (event, u, triad), error control on the event (absolute tol × r) and u (tol × max(1, |u^μ|)), default tol 1e-10; the triad follows, re-orthonormalised every 10 steps (u renormalised first, then Gram–Schmidt); the step is also bounded by 0.05 rad of attitude rotation. The plan allowed RK4 or embedded RK45; DP5 meets the 1e-8-per-orbit budget with ~280 steps per orbit.
- 2026-11-18 — **Christoffel symbols** (`js/physics/christoffel.js`): 4th-order central differences of g_ab in the three spatial coordinates, step 1e-4 × the distance from the centre (the plan's 1e-5 gave larger rounding: 1e-16/h); checked against Schwarzschild's analytic symbols to 3e-12 (all 40 components; zero ones to 3e-14).
